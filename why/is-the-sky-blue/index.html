Why is the sky blue?

Quick answer: Sunlight is white, but air molecules scatter short wavelengths (blue) far more than long ones (red). That scattered blue light reaches your eyes from every direction — so the sky looks blue.

Rayleigh scattering

Named after Lord Rayleigh, this effect makes scattering intensity proportional to 1/λ⁴. Blue light (~450 nm) scatters roughly 5–6× more than red light (~700 nm). So blue is redirected across the sky, while red passes through more directly.

Related questions

Why is the sunset red? At sunset, sunlight travels through much more atmosphere. Blue is scattered away entirely, leaving the longer wavelengths — orange and red — to reach your eyes.

Why isn't the sky violet? Violet scatters even more than blue, but the Sun emits less violet, and our eyes are more sensitive to blue.

Why is space black? There's nothing to scatter light. No atmosphere, no blue glow.
